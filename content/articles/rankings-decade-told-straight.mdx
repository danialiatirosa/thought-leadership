<h3>Asia's share of Top 100 in the World University Rankings more than doubled, driven by China</h3>

<Finding index={1} num="2×" trend="up">
Asia's share of the Times Higher Education (THE) World University Rankings (WUR) Top 100 grew from 9% in 2016 to 21% in 2027. QS shows a smaller shift, from 20% to 23%, because it leans more on reputation surveys, and reputation moves slowly.

<h3>US is losing ground, especially below the Top 100</h3>

<Finding index={3} num="−2 / −26" tone="neg" trend="down">
The US's WUR Top 100 university count fell: 39 to 37 on THE, 32 to 26 on QS. Universities close to the top (Harvard, MIT and Stanford) hold almost the same rank as a decade ago. In the WUR Top 500, the drop is noticeable: the US lost 26 institutions on THE, the largest net decline of any country this decade (see chart).

<h3>The Gulf is a second growth engine, after China</h3>

<Finding index={4} num="+8 / +6" trend="up">
Saudi Arabia (+8 institutions) is the next-biggest gainer in the WUR Top 500 after China, and the UAE (+6 institutions) is close behind.
</Finding>

<Finding index={5} num="258" trend="up">
Khalifa University's QS rank climbed 258 places this decade, from the 401-410 band in 2017 to rank 147 in 2027. That's the largest gain of any UAE university. </Finding>

<Finding index={6} num="63" trend="up">

</Finding>

<p style={{ marginTop: 24 }}>
<strong>The decade's biggest shifts came from four countries. China, Saudi Arabia and the UAE are growth stories, and the US is where the erosion sits.</strong>
</p>

<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>China added 25 universities to THE's Top 500 over the decade and quadrupled its Top 100 count, from 2 to 8.</strong> The rise lines up with the Double First-Class programme.
    <ul>
      <li>Double First-Class funds about 140 universities to build world-class research capacity, in five-year cycles since 2016.</li>
      <li>Its funding is conditional: after the first cycle, the Ministry of Education publicly warned or revoked 16 disciplines across 15 universities.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Saudi Arabia's presence in THE's Top 500 rose from one university to nine.</strong> Vision 2030 set a target of at least five universities in the global Top 200.
    <ul>
      <li>On QS, three Saudi universities are already in the Top 200: King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM, 63), King Saud University (107) and King Abdulaziz University (200).</li>
      <li>On THE, only KFUPM (176) makes the Top 200.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>The UAE's presence in THE's Top 500 increased from zero to six universities</strong>, the second-largest gain in the Gulf after Saudi Arabia. They are:
    <ul>
      <li>Khalifa University and United Arab Emirates University (both 235)</li>
      <li>Abu Dhabi University and the University of Sharjah (both 301–325)</li>
      <li>Ajman University (376–400)</li>
      <li>Al Ain University (401–450)</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>The US is still the largest cohort in both rankings, but its university count in THE's Top 500 fell from 122 to 96 between 2016 and 2027.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>The decline was concentrated among public universities outside the Top 100. Harvard, MIT and Stanford have held their positions.</li>
      <li>Several prominent private universities have declined in the QS rankings. Princeton dropped from 11th to 27th, Brown from 49th to 66th, and Dartmouth from 158th to 270th.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>


<ul style={{ marginTop: 24 }}>
  <li>
    <strong>Singapore did not grow its presence in the Top 100.</strong> Its THE count remained at two institutions, NUS and NTU, throughout the decade.<sup>†</sup>
    <ul>
      <li>NUS and NTU do not appear on the risers list, because they were already elite in 2017 (24th and 54th on THE).</li>
      <li>Over the decade, NUS climbed from QS 12th to 10th, and NTU's bigger move was on THE, from 54th to 28th.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>

<ul style={{ marginTop: 32 }}>
  <li>
    <strong>The ranking tables themselves have grown over the last decade.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>Between 2017 and 2027, the number of universities ranked increased by 1,317 on THE and by 588 on QS.</li>
      <li>124 universities withdrew from THE rankings between 2020 and 2026. Some withdrawals were protests: seven Indian Institutes of Technology announced in 2020 that they would not take part, citing a lack of transparency in THE's methods.</li>
      <li>The score needed for a Top 100 place has risen. On THE, the 100th-ranked university scored 58.0 in 2016 and 66.6 in 2027. A university that kept the same score over that period would have fallen down the table without getting any worse.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>

<p style={{ marginTop: 32 }}>
<strong>The biggest risers are linked to robust national funding programmes.</strong>
</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Seven of the 15 risers are mainland Chinese universities</strong>, all funded under the Double First-Class programme.</li>
  <li><strong>Two more risers are Hong Kong universities</strong> (Hong Kong Polytechnic and the Chinese University of Hong Kong), funded by Hong Kong's University Grants Committee.</li>
  <li><strong>Four are Korean universities funded by Brain Korea 21</strong> (Yonsei, KAIST, Sungkyunkwan and Korea University), a graduate-research programme running since 1999. Korea's climb is two and a half decades of steady investment.</li>
  <li><strong>The other two are Germany's Charité</strong>, a specialist medical university funded by the Excellence Strategy, <strong>and Italy's University of Bologna</strong>.</li>
</ul>

<p style={{ marginTop: 32 }}>
One pattern holds across the entire risers list. <strong>The universities that climbed are the ones whose governments committed to sustained funding, paired with strong accountability mechanisms.</strong> The gains came with a lag: China named its first funded universities in 2017, and its biggest climbs followed from 2019 onwards. National commitment eventually shows up in the rankings, regardless of region; how quickly depends on how much infrastructure the system already had.
</p>

<h3 style={{ display: 'flex', alignItems: 'center', gap: 10, marginTop: 40 }}><Flag country="United Arab Emirates" width={24} /> United Arab Emirates</h3>

<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>Khalifa University is the UAE's biggest riser.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>Its QS rank moved from the 401-410 band in 2017<sup>†</sup> to 147 in 2027, second in the Gulf only to Qatar University (393rd to 109th). On THE, it moved from the 501-600 band to 235.</li>
      <li>The climb followed a 2017 merger with the Petroleum Institute and the Masdar Institute, creating a single flagship.</li>
      <li>Its board is appointed by Abu Dhabi Executive Council resolution and includes executives from Mubadala, ADNOC, ADQ, ADIA and the UAE Space Agency.</li>
      <li>The university attributes its rise to strategic investment in research, talent and global partnerships, and its hiring reflects this: it ranks 12th in the world on QS's international faculty measure.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Four of Abu Dhabi's government-backed universities do not appear in either ranking as UAE entries.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>NYU Abu Dhabi, IIT Delhi–Abu Dhabi and Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi are satellite campuses, ranked under New York University (QS 58, THE 29), IIT Delhi (QS 118; not in THE's table) and Sorbonne University (QS 73; withdrew from THE from the 2027 edition).</li>
      <li>MBZUAI is in neither table: both require undergraduate teaching, and its first undergraduates only arrived in 2025.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>


<h3 style={{ display: 'flex', alignItems: 'center', gap: 10 }}><Flag country="Saudi Arabia" width={24} /> Saudi Arabia</h3>

<p className="no-indent">
Saudi Arabia spread its funding across a broad base of public universities in Riyadh, Jeddah and the Eastern Province. Its THE WUR Top 500 count went from 1 to 9, and its QS count from 3 to 6.
</p>

<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM) leads the group at QS 63</strong>, the first Arab university to break into the QS Top 100.
    <ul>
      <li>Since gaining independence from the government in 2020, under president Dr. Muhammad Al-Saggaf, it has built more than 20 research centres, added 43 undergraduate concentrations and grown its postdoc base roughly tenfold.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>KAUST doesn't appear in either ranking.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>It's graduate-only, and both systems require undergraduate teaching.</li>
      <li>Instead, it has topped THE's Arab University Rankings in the 2024 to 2026 editions, while KFUPM leads QS's equivalent regional table.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>King Abdulaziz University is the warning sign.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>It hit an outright THE rank of 101 in 2023, then fell to the 351–400 band by 2025 and to 401–450 in 2027.</li>
      <li>Its rise rested partly on incentivising international researchers to list it as a secondary affiliation<sup>‡</sup>, which inflated citations without adding research capacity. When THE cut the weight of citation impact in 2024, the rank fell with it.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>

<p className="no-indent">
<strong>A rank built on research capability and industry ties survives a methodology change. One built on citation engineering doesn't.</strong>
</p>

<h3 style={{ display: 'flex', alignItems: 'center', gap: 10 }}><Flag country="Qatar" width={24} /> Qatar</h3>

<p className="no-indent">
Qatar's first bet was branch campuses, rather than a flagship or broad funding. Education City hosts Georgetown, Northwestern, Weill Cornell Medicine, Texas A&M<sup>§</sup>, Carnegie Mellon, Virginia Commonwealth University and HEC Paris, all ranked under their home institutions rather than as Qatari entries. Qatar is now also building top-ranked universities of its own.
</p>

<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>Hamad Bin Khalifa University (HBKU) is now the Gulf's highest-ranked university on THE.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>It debuted in 2027 at 126, ahead of every Saudi and UAE university.</li>
      <li>It joined QS in 2024 and sits at 213.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Qatar University has made the largest QS climb of any Gulf university.</strong>
    <ul>
      <li>It rose from 393rd in 2017 to 109th in 2027, making it the country's top-ranked university on QS.</li>
      <li>On THE, it has held a Top 500 place since 2018, now at 222.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>

<SectionHeader
  number="04"
  kicker="Learnings & Implications"
  title="Rankings reward clear strategy."
/>

<p className="lead">
This decade's data speaks most directly to governments and sovereign funders, who decide where the money goes. The points below turn that data into funding decisions: what to target, how to structure support, and where new risks such as AI are changing the calculus.
</p>

<div className="grid grid-cols-[minmax(180px,240px)_1fr] gap-x-8 items-start pt-6 border-t border-green/25 first:pt-0 first:border-t-0 max-[640px]:grid-cols-1 max-[640px]:gap-y-2">
  <div className="font-serif text-[18px] font-semibold text-green leading-[1.25]">Rankings reward a narrow set of outcomes</div>
  <div className="text-[17px] leading-[1.55] text-ink/85">Rankings count research output, internationalisation and reputation. They don't measure social mobility, regional impact or alignment with national priorities. Funding a university to climb the rankings means funding what rankings measure, not its overall quality.</div>
</div>

<div className="grid grid-cols-[minmax(180px,240px)_1fr] gap-x-8 items-start pt-6 border-t border-green/25 max-[640px]:grid-cols-1 max-[640px]:gap-y-2" style={{ marginTop: 24 }}>
  <div className="font-serif text-[18px] font-semibold text-green leading-[1.25]">Know which ranking you're measured against</div>
  <div className="text-[17px] leading-[1.55] text-ink/85">QS and THE often disagree, and both also publish regional and specialist rankings, each with its own rules and winners. KAUST, for instance, is excluded from both flagship rankings, yet it has topped THE's Arab University Rankings three years running. Set targets against the ranking that matters for your goals.</div>
</div>

<div className="grid grid-cols-[minmax(180px,240px)_1fr] gap-x-8 items-start pt-6 border-t border-green/25 max-[640px]:grid-cols-1 max-[640px]:gap-y-2" style={{ marginTop: 24 }}>
  <div className="font-serif text-[18px] font-semibold text-green leading-[1.25]">A numbered, time-bound target works</div>
  <div className="text-[17px] leading-[1.55] text-ink/85">Saudi Arabia set one: at least five universities in the global Top 200 by 2030. It focused ministerial effort and created accountability, and it is delivering: three Saudi universities are now in the QS Top 200, and KFUPM became the first Arab university in the QS Top 100.</div>
</div>

<div className="grid grid-cols-[minmax(180px,240px)_1fr] gap-x-8 items-start pt-6 border-t border-green/25 max-[640px]:grid-cols-1 max-[640px]:gap-y-2" style={{ marginTop: 24 }}>
  <div className="font-serif text-[18px] font-semibold text-green leading-[1.25]">Funding an existing university pays off faster than building one</div>
  <div className="text-[17px] leading-[1.55] text-ink/85">A new university takes more than a decade to register: KAUST (founded 2009) and MBZUAI (2019) still don't appear in either flagship ranking. Funding an existing one shows results faster: China's Double First-Class universities and Khalifa University climbed rankings significantly within five to seven years.</div>
</div>

<div className="grid grid-cols-[minmax(180px,240px)_1fr] gap-x-8 items-start pt-6 border-t border-green/25 max-[640px]:grid-cols-1 max-[640px]:gap-y-2" style={{ marginTop: 24 }}>
  <div className="font-serif text-[18px] font-semibold text-green leading-[1.25]">Keeping international talent lifts rankings</div>
  <div className="text-[17px] leading-[1.55] text-ink/85">Retaining international talent feeds the rankings directly. QS gives 15% of its score to international faculty, international students and international research networks, and THE gives 7.5% to its International Outlook pillar. Graduates who stay and keep co-authoring papers also build citation scores over time. The UAE Golden Visa, the UK Graduate Route, the German Blue Card and the Singapore TechPass all work as research-retention tools.</div>

  <div className="font-serif text-[18px] font-semibold text-green leading-[1.25]">Branch campuses pay off through local partners</div>
  <div className="text-[17px] leading-[1.55] text-ink/85">A branch campus is ranked under its home university, not the host country. Its value comes through collaboration: its researchers co-author with local universities, lifting their research output and international-network scores. Qatar's Education City is the clearest example. Georgetown, Northwestern, Weill Cornell Medicine and others run campuses there, but none appears as a Qatari entry.</div>

  <div className="font-serif text-[18px] font-semibold text-green leading-[1.25]">AI brings both upside and integrity risk</div>

But the same tools make it cheaper to mass-produce low-quality papers and inflate citation counts, the kind of citation engineering that lifted and then sank King Abdulaziz University's rank. Universities with strong research-integrity and data systems are best placed to capture the upside.</div>
